# Enterprise adoption of AI coding agents and SDLC restructuring (2025–2026)

Method note: this is based on web search result snippets and summaries. Direct page fetches to gartner.com, ciodive.com and americanbanker.com were blocked by the session's network policy, so some figures are taken from secondary coverage of the primary source. Each claim is tagged as follows. [Company] means a statement by the adopting company: executive, earnings call or blog. [Vendor] means a vendor, partner or system-integrator case study or announcement. [Independent] means news or analyst reporting. [Secondary/weak] means an aggregator or blog. Today's date is 2026-11-10.

## 1. Which large companies have publicly described agentic SDLC programs, and with what results

### Takeaway
Big tech publishes headline "share of code written by AI" numbers. Google says 75% of new code as of April 2026. Microsoft gave 20–30% in April 2025. Meta gave a target of about 50% for 2026. Banks publish smaller productivity numbers: JPMorgan 10–20%, Citi Copilot 5–15%, Goldman about 20% with assistants. Banks have moved from copilots to supervised autonomous agents, mostly Devin, at the scale of thousands of engineers, but agents are not allowed to deploy. Nearly all figures are self-reported, and none are independently audited.

### Cited Findings
**Big tech**

That is up from about 50% in fall 2025 and about 25% in October 2024. One outlet gives the prior figure as about 30%, which conflicts with the 50% reported elsewhere. Google has not disclosed rejection or rewrite rates. ### Inferences
- There are two kinds of metric. Tech firms report "share of code AI-generated", while banks report "productivity %" or "hours saved". Neither is comparable across firms, and neither is audited.
- The pattern in banks for 2025–26 is a copilot for everyone (5–20% gains), followed by supervised autonomous agents (Devin) for bounded tasks such as legacy updates, migrations and tests. Humans own the work, and agents may not deploy.
- Service integrators (Accenture, Cognizant, Infosys, TCS) are becoming the delivery channel for agent tooling in regulated clients, and are also exposed to revenue cannibalization.

### Gaps

## 2. How enterprises structure adoption: CoEs, platform engineering, approved tools, pilot to scale, champions

### Takeaway
The documented pattern has several stages. It starts with a time-boxed pilot that has measurement, such as ANZ's 6 weeks or JPMorgan's in-house tool. That is followed by a broad licence rollout with an AI or Engineering Productivity CoE, champions, train-the-trainer and dashboards. A measurement platform such as DX tracks impact. Integrators often run the enablement. Autonomous agents are layered on later for bounded tasks, with humans in the loop. McKinsey, Forrester and Gartner all argue that real gains need process and role redesign, not tools alone.

### Cited Findings
- A global bank with 6,500+ Copilot users had licences but no scalable adoption. Xebia ran a structured enablement program with champions, governance, community and train-the-trainer after a successful pilot. [Vendor] — [Xebia](https://xebia.com/customer-stories/from-license-to-leverage-scaling-github-copilot-for-6500-users-at-a-leading-global-bank)
- Persistent Systems set up an Engineering Productivity CoE with a rollout framework and role-based enablement tracks for Admin, Developer and Champion. It covered about 40 scrum teams and 320 engineers and claims a 20% uplift and 40% effort savings. [Vendor] — [Persistent](https://www.persistent.com/client-success/from-ai-pilots-to-enterprise-productivity-20-uplift-and-40-effort-savings/)
- Saxo Bank built an AI CoE with AI champions and visible leadership sponsorship. [Vendor] — [Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en/customers/story/24389-saxo-bank-github-copilot)
- CIBC used upskilling sessions, "Show & Tell" sessions and community spaces, starting with 1,800+ developers and expanding to all. [Vendor] — [Deloitte](https://www.deloitte.com/ca/en/about/story/impact/cibc.html)
- A UK bank ("FinanceCo") tracked every AI use case end to end against its risk appetite and governance frameworks. It flagged over-reliance risk where developers lack the skill to challenge AI output. [Independent research: CIPD/IFOW] — [CIPD](https://www.cipd.org/en/knowledge/case-studies/ai-developer-collaboration)
- ANZ ran a controlled 6-week Copilot experiment measuring productivity, quality, security and sentiment before scaling. [Independent] — [iTnews](https://www.itnews.com.au/news/anz-examines-uneven-benefits-from-ai-augmented-coding-609907)
- Several banks built tools in-house instead of buying them: JPMorgan's in-house coding assistant and Morgan Stanley's DevGen.AI, trained on its own codebase and proprietary languages. This reflects data-protection and IP constraints. — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/jpmorgan-engineers-efficiency-jumps-much-190410471.html); [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/a-team-of-engineers-saved-morgan-stanley-more-than-280000-hours-this-year-the-bank-says-their-tool-wont-take-jobs/)
- Booking.com uses the DX developer-experience platform to measure GenAI impact across about 3,000 developers. [Vendor] — [DX](https://getdx.com/customers/booking-uses-dx-to-measure-impact-of-genai/)
- McKinsey ("Beyond the copilot") found that organizations that redesigned processes before adopting AI were more than 2x as likely to report productivity gains above 20% than those that layered AI onto existing ways of working. Role and way-of-working redesign drives the biggest gains. [Independent analyst] — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/technology-media-and-telecommunications/our-insights/beyond-the-copilot-scaling-the-agentic-product-development-life-cycle)
- McKinsey ("Rewiring software delivery for the agentic era", 2026) describes a "daily sprint" model in which humans and agents work around the clock. It reports 3–5x productivity and up to 60% smaller teams at some companies. McKinsey's Martin Harrysson calls for smaller, highly leveraged teams that supervise agents through execution. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/industries/software-and-IT-services/our-insights/rewiring-software-delivery-for-the-agentic-era); quote via [CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/enterprises-bet-coding-agents-despite-ROI/830943/)
- McKinsey ("When AI becomes part of the workflow") says most organizations are still in "Horizon 1", experimenting with tools without rethinking how software is built end to end. It describes a Sonar pilot with agents across the lifecycle, from research and ideation to development and testing. — [McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/overview/when-ai-becomes-part-of-the-workflow-redesigning-how-software-gets-built)
- Gartner predicts that 60% of organizations will adopt smaller software engineering teams at scale by 2029, up from 15% in 2026. It warns that by 2028, firms that use AI to eliminate junior roles will weaken their talent pipelines. — [People Matters](https://sea.peoplemattersglobal.com/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/60percent-of-organisations-to-adopt-smaller-software-engineering-teams-by-2029-gartner-50748)

### Inferences
- Approved-tools lists and procurement: banks often run more than one tool, for example Citi with Copilot plus Devin, or build their own when data residency or IP is a concern. Large IT providers spread across Microsoft, Anthropic and Cognition.
- The "platform engineering owns agent infrastructure" model is plausible and widely discussed, but I found no primary enterprise source describing it explicitly. See Gaps.

### Gaps
- I found no named large enterprise publicly describing a platform-engineering team that owns agent runtimes, sandboxes or MCP gateways. A follow-up search is needed, for example on GitHub Universe, re:Invent or Anthropic customer talks.
- No public data on procurement terms such as data retention or zero-data-retention clauses at specific banks.

## 3. Governance in regulated environments

### Takeaway
Governance shows up in practice as human-in-the-loop ownership, a ban on agent self-deployment (Citi), use-case tracking against risk appetite (UK bank), and in-house or private deployments. No regulator has issued guidance specifically on AI-generated source code. The working principle in EU finance is that a firm must be able to prove the origin, review and approval of every change. DORA ICT-risk and third-party rules and the EU AI Act logging rules apply indirectly.

### Cited Findings
- Citi does not allow agents to deploy code, and a human developer remains in charge of every Devin task. — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/citi-is-rolling-out-agentic-ai-to-its-40-000-developers)
- Goldman says Devin is supervised by human employees, and engineers are expected to specify problems and review AI work. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/14/goldman-sachs-ai-powered-software-engineer-devin-new-employee-increase-productivity-fears-of-job-replacement/)
- Morgan Stanley says full code translation is immature, so humans rewrite code from AI-produced specifications. — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/a-team-of-engineers-saved-morgan-stanley-more-than-280000-hours-this-year-the-bank-says-their-tool-wont-take-jobs/)
- One practitioner view (an AI-native modernization consultancy) is that AI-generated code is neither allowed nor forbidden as a category in banks. What is allowed is code whose origin, review and approval can be proven. This extends to vendors and outsourced engineering partners, so it becomes a third-party governance question. DORA and the EU AI Act are in scope for EU entities. [Vendor blog] — [CreateQ](https://www.createq.com/insights/is-ai-generated-code-allowed-in-a-bank-a-traceability-answer)
- DORA has applied since 17 January 2025. Vendors read Article 9 (ICT security) as requiring records that allow reconstruction of events, including AI-influenced actions. This is an interpretation, not regulator text. [Vendor] — [DeepInspect](https://www.deepinspect.ai/blog/dora-ai-compliance-for-banks); [Velatir](https://www.velatir.com/blog/dora-eu-ai-act-overlap-banks)
- Under the EU AI Act, Article 19 requires log retention of at least 6 months for high-risk systems. A Backbase post reports that the Annex III high-risk deadline moved from August 2026 to December 2027 under the "digital omnibus"; this is not confirmed against an official source. Coding assistants are generally not Annex III high-risk. [Vendor] — [Backbase](https://www.backbase.com/blog/ai-vendor-evaluation-private-bank); [Velatir](https://www.velatir.com/blog/dora-eu-ai-act-overlap-banks)
- Vendors argue that audit logs written by the same application that took the action are weak evidence and should be generated independently. [Vendor] — [DeepInspect](https://www.deepinspect.ai/blog/dora-third-party-ai-risk)
- T-Systems describes its AI code-generation tool as compliant with the EU AI Act. [Company] — [T-Systems](https://www.t-systems.com/in/en/insights/newsroom/expert-blogs/software-development-with-the-help-of-artificial-intelligence-1069834)
- Review burden: in the Harness State of Engineering Excellence 2026 report, 89% of engineering leaders say AI improved productivity, but 81% say developers spend more time on manual code review. [Vendor survey via Independent] — [iStart](https://istart.com.au/news-items/ai-coding-runs-into-real-world-limits/)

### Inferences
- In practice, the control set in regulated firms maps onto existing SDLC controls. Segregation of duties is kept because an agent cannot approve or deploy its own change. Change-management evidence (who requested, who reviewed, who approved) is extended to agent identities. AI vendors go through DORA third-party risk management and the register of information. These are inferences from the cited practices, not documented rules.
- The rising review burden (81% in Harness) is the main operational bottleneck for governance.

### Gaps
- No primary sources were found on SOX or PCI treatment of AI-generated code, on model-risk-management (SR 11-7) applied to coding agents, or on licence and IP provenance scanning policies at named banks.
- No guidance from ESAs, BaFin, FCA, OCC or the AI Office specifically on AI-written code was found.

## 4. Legacy modernization with agents (COBOL, Java, mainframe)

### Takeaway
Modernization is the most measurable enterprise use case. Examples include Amazon's Java upgrades (about 4,500 developer-years), Morgan Stanley's COBOL-to-specification work (9M lines and 280,000 hours in 2025), and Itaú's mainframe discovery (3 months down to 2 weeks). Most outcomes are self-reported or published by vendors.

### Cited Findings
- Morgan Stanley's DevGen.AI launched in January 2025. It is built in-house on OpenAI GPT and trained on the bank's code, including proprietary languages. It translates COBOL and other legacy code into plain-English specifications for developers to rewrite. Mike Pizzi said it reviewed 9 million lines in 2025 and saved about 280,000 hours. It is available to about 15,000 developers. Executives say it won't replace developers. One syndicated copy wrongly dates this to 2023. [Company, via WSJ/BI] — [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.nl/a-team-of-engineers-saved-morgan-stanley-more-than-280000-hours-this-year-the-bank-says-their-tool-wont-take-jobs/); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/morgan-stanley-builds-ai-tool-that-fixes-major-coding-issue/492697); [Le Monde Informatique](https://www.lemondeinformatique.fr/actualites/lire-comment-morgan-stanley-se-sert-de-la-genai-pour-bouter-cobol-97061.html)
- Amazon's Java 8/11 to 17 migration: see section 1. About 30,000 apps, 50 developer-days down to hours, about 4,500 developer-years and $260M per year (2024). [Company/Vendor] — [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/amazon-q-developer-just-reached-a-260-million-dollar-milestone)
- Itaú Unibanco used AWS Transform and Kiro to reverse-engineer COBOL, IMS, CICS and Db2 systems. Application discovery fell from 3 months to 2 weeks. Kiro handles conversion to Java or Python. [Vendor] — [AWS case study](https://aws.amazon.com/solutions/case-studies/itau-aws-transform-mainframe/)
- Western Union and Unum, on AWS: 2.5M lines of COBOL converted in about 1.5 hours, and Western Union's project timeline cut from 3+ months to 6 weeks. [Secondary summary of a vendor talk] — [ZenML LLMOps DB](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/agentic-ai-framework-for-mainframe-modernization-at-scale)
- AWS Transform for mainframe offers both deterministic refactoring and an agentic "reimagine" path. AWS also has a blog on combining AWS Transform with Claude Code. AWS and Thought Machine launched an offering that converts mainframe COBOL into Vault-based products. [Vendor] — [AWS blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/migration-and-modernization/reimagining-mainframe-applications-with-aws-transform-and-claude-code/); [Softprom](https://softprom.com/aws-thought-machine-ai-core-banking-migration)
- Danske Bank and Fiserv presented agentic mainframe modernization at re:Invent 2025, but only session descriptions were found, with no outcomes. — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/kazuya_dev/aws-reinvent-2025-digital-transformation-excellence-using-agentic-ai-from-vision-to-victory-mam204-1042)
- Practitioners stress that COBOL and domain experts remain necessary to define what each transformation should do. — [ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/software/development/you-need-those-experts-to-even-define-what-these-transformations-are-cobol-developers-will-always-be-needed-even-as-ai-takes-the-lead-on-modernization-projects)
- Goldman's Devin is aimed first at legacy code updates. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/07/14/goldman-sachs-ai-powered-software-engineer-devin-new-employee-increase-productivity-fears-of-job-replacement/)
- Booking.com uses agents for monolith-to-microservices migrations. — [Sourcegraph](https://sourcegraph.com/blog/virtual-code-ai-summit-recap)

### Inferences
- Modernization is the beachhead for autonomous agents in regulated firms. The work is bounded, has clear specifications and tests, can be measured as hours or developer-years saved, and keeps humans at review gates.
- The most mature pattern is AI-assisted understanding: discovery, documentation and specification extraction. Fully automated translation is less mature, as Morgan Stanley says explicitly.

### Gaps
- No independent post-migration data on defect rates or cost was found for any of these programs.

## 5. Analyst views: Gartner, Forrester, McKinsey, BCG, Deloitte

### Takeaway
Analysts agree that the gain depends on redesigning the whole lifecycle and operating model, not only coding. Their numbers diverge widely. Deloitte and some McKinsey case work claim 30–35% or 3–5x gains, while McKinsey's 2026 survey found only about 3% average gains, with 30% of teams seeing productivity fall. Gartner now also warns that AI coding costs may exceed developer salaries by 2028.

### Cited Findings
- Gartner, 1 July 2025: "AI-native software engineering" is one of the top 6 strategic trends. It predicts 90% of enterprise software engineers will use AI code assistants by 2028, up from less than 14% in early 2024. Its earlier 2024 forecast was 75%. ### Inferences
- The analyst consensus amounts to an Amdahl's-law argument. Speeding up coding alone yields under 10% at team level, so value requires agents across requirements, testing, review and release, plus smaller agent-supervising teams.
- The emerging counter-signals are cost (Gartner's token-spend warning), review overload (Harness) and mixed measured outcomes (McKinsey's 30% of teams with falling productivity). Enterprise programs should therefore build FinOps for agents and measurement in from the start.
